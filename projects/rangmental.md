# The Dimensions of Warmth and Competence, Social Ranking, and Mental Health — RangMental

Why do some people gain more power and influence than others, and how does their social standing affect their mental health and other aspects of well-being?
In this project, we explore why and how people achieve different positions in dominance and prestige hierarchies.
We investigate how perceptions of warmth and competence shape these processes and how different ways of navigating social hierarchies affect mental health and psychological well-being across everyday life, clinical settings, and sports. Using experiments and repeated assessments of everyday experiences, we aim to better understand the complex links between social rank and mental health.

## Project description

The formation of social hierarchies occurs spontaneously and is reflected in different levels of individuals’ influence within a group.
A higher social rank (SR) brings greater influence and resources and is generally associated with subjective well-being and mental health.
This relationship is complex and largely unexplored.
The dual-strategies theory of SR distinguishes dominance, based on coercive respect through the aggressive use of power, and prestige, which arises from voluntary respect earned through contribution to the group.
Submissive behavior (acceptance of lower SR) or strategies for increasing SR can affect mental health and well-being through the satisfaction of basic psychological needs for autonomy, competence, and relatedness.

This project builds on previous research done by the principal investigator on emotional crying as a form of submissive behavior and the application of warmth (WA) and competence (CO) dimensions in explaining SR regulation behaviors.
WA includes features such as social closeness, while CO encompasses different types of abilities.
The association between WA, CO, and crying received initial empirical support, but their role in explaining other submissive and higher SR-seeking behaviors remains unknown.
Crucially, in order to understand the consequences of SR regulation behaviors for mental health, it is necessary to understand their determinants.

The main aim of this project is to examine whether the causes and consequences of SR regulation behaviors can be explained by using the WA-CO model.
The theory developed in crying research will be applied to different types of SR regulation behaviors in order to investigate their effects on mental health and well-being.
We will conduct experimental and correlational studies, as well as the ecological momentary assessment (EMA) method combined with psychophysiological assessment, in order to gain a better understanding of the SR dynamics in real-time and across different contexts.
